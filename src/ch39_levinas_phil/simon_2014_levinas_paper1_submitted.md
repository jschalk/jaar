## What does it mean that I own a car? 

A Levinasian way to understand my car and a car way to understand Levinas

#### The Thread of Phenomenological Ethics

What is Phenomenological Ethics? Let’s answer that in the phenomenological way of describing experience: I first heard of Phenomenological Ethics when Dr. Simon of my university spoke of it to the class I was sitting in on. He spoke with great passion about how ethical discourse, within the academic institutions at least, was/is lacking. This lacking comes basically (by my interpretation) in the form of excessive conceptualization (this is my experience through my interpretive lens) where virtue ethics, deontological, and utilitarian ethics are all putting the all people into one box. Importantly for Levinas and Phenomenological Ethics there is always two boxes: The one I am in and the one everyone else is in. (There is actually both absolute separation and absolute connectedness but for now only the separateness is introduced.) I am always in my box looking at the other box from it. How does this work? Let’s follow the adventures of Levinas and my car throughout a single day.

Throughout this story I’ll be introducing the three main reductions required by Phenomenological ethics simplified here as bracketing my subjectivity, recognizing the intersubjective, and finally considering the third, all the people not in the room, and thus outside the conversation. 

I’m having fun with this paper but it is important to remember that one of the initiatives of Phenomenological Ethics is provide a more excellent ethical framework for conceptualize moral problems. How we approach problems in the world often decides how we ‘solve’ them. When it is decided that everyone is in one box that actualizes as a situation where everyone is in their single box, absolutely isolated. When two boxes(people) interact there is no merging or sharing boxes, one box must be logically proven better! On box must win and be on top of the other box (person). This is the way of war. Consider how often war is explained to be a solution, because, damn it, we just can’t think of anything else. Says the legitimate strawman I’ve created here, “I have war because on box is on top and one box is on bottom and that box won’t let me get on top without coercion.” This is wrong because there’s is plenty of room in both boxes! The intention of this work is a serious move against never sharing boxes.

### The first reduction: bracketing my subjective

Given that I am in one box and all others are in the other box my first step is to ask what it means to be in my box. What’s my subjective experience?

This morning I woke up to my alarm and turned it off many times. I got up and began my routine that involves making oatmeal for breakfast and sandwiches for the day. I do this in safety and comfort compared to many places in the world because I am an American citizen and I live in El Paso. I am in good legal standing and feel confident that my government serves/protects me (at least a little) and doesn’t subjugate me. This makes me somewhat unique in the world and perhaps even among my neighbors in my sometimes poor part of town. I am also a French citizen which grants me access to the European style of life. Also my family is fairly large and loves me. I grew up with these privileges (although some might say blinders) and consider myself a fairly mental healthy person, an inheritor of Western Philosophical thought and more precisely American progressivism and French gauchiste politics. I was born during the time of the American Empire and I’m probably unable to bracket out completely my Americaness from anything I do. 

All this and more colors my subjective position in the world and thus how I write this paper or consider the ethical problem of a gas-consuming car. These things I try to bracket, to hold apart, while I consider the car. “I BRACKET my conditions to provide a description of this or that situation with the language and the training that is naturally mine at this particular place and time in my life.” (What is.., p 2)

More subjective: my language has become progressively more Levinasian from years of considering Levinas. This is a change from my previous mode of thought. I have an entire education since elementary school drowned in the metaphysics of analysis which Knows breaking things apart then considering them whole is The Way to Knowledge. I narratively consider my story to be changing, often better but I’m not really sure, and to support that change I’m currently still processing Otherwise than Being. In this immediate moment I’m consider in particular the concept of getting the saying from the said. Tough stuff.

In the morning I wake up and get in my car

#### I don’t clean my car - the same

In the morning I begin my journey in the car. My car is incredibly dirty by the standards of most cars I have been in. It is dirty in my own way: no food or organic matter, just dust every, playing cards and papers lying about. Because the car is the only property I own outright it is the only place where I decide everything absolutely. What is to be cleaned or not cleaned, what is to be put where. Anything I don’t want in my car I throw out and it is no longer mine. Anything I wish to bring in I may. (this is of course to be considered in context. I cannot put bales of cocaine in my car even though it is my property). Analogous to this is how the same for Levinas represents metaphysical absoluteness. Whatever it is that never changes. No matter what happens there is always ‘the same’. In man-body dualism it is the soul that can never be destroyed without death. The known fact I am me. Even if all my atoms change ‘the same’ is the the same. If I cut off my arm the same is unchanged. if I become faceless in the dark the same is unchanged, if I sustain a brain injury but do not die the same is unchanged. It is a place where no one or thing can clean.

#### I never know who’s parked around me - the other

Every time I look for the car it is parked the same place I last put it. But all around the other cars can change. I never know exactly where the other cars are going to be parked or what they are going to look like or whether their insides are clean of dust but have old candy bars stuck under a seat. With ants all over them…ugh that’s nasty. 

The other cars here are analogous to ‘the other’ in that I can never know exactly everything about them. In my car the window is cleaned how I like it, the seat is moved to where I am comfortable and the radio has my settings. But I look out my window and I don’t know what I will see. I open my door and I don’t know what I’ll hear. From inside my car I look out onto the street. This is how I know where I am as a driver! Someone in front of a map can decide where I am on their map but I am actually driving and I look out subjectively to know where I am. From the same to the other is where I drive. It is the metaphysical basis for Levinas. “The knot tied in subjectivity...signifies an allegiance of the same to the other…[is] preliminary to all consciousness.” (Otherwise, p25) 

#### My doesn’t look good - thematization as constant

Now my car is my car and I have described it as dirty with dust and yet clean of food. The dust is from of years of never vacuuming, wiping down, or ‘detailing’ my car. The dust is made up of all the particles that fell off people who have come in and out of my car, all the objects that have come in and out of my car, and all the winds, often sandy in El Paso, that have been part of the story of the car. The story of what the dust is in its entirety is infinitely long and impossible to know. In simplification we call it dust. In the same way I thematize the same (myself) and the other (people not me) so as to have a practical hold on the world. Even though this is a reduction: the story of the dust is not just that it is dust, the story is an infinitely long story that’s been arbitrarily cut off. And yet this is necessary to function in our subjectivity in the world. Levinas doesn’t deny we have to thematize. We just need to know its never the truth complete.

> Subjectivity qua consciousness can thus be interpreted as the articulation of an ontological event, as one of the mysterious ways in which its "act of being" is deployed. Being a theme, being intelligible or open, possessing oneself, the moment of having in being - all that is articulated in the movement of essence, getting rid of itself and finding itself out of a conceptual principle, an dpxv, in its thematic exposition, being thus carries on its affair of being. (Otherwise, p99)

Levinas is here describing how when we thematize people like the dust is thematized there will always be a ‘thematic explosion’ that will surprise us. Maybe the dust won’t surprise us but the other always will. 

#### I don’t lock my car - welcome

I do not lock my car doors most every night. It is considered normal in most of the Western World to always keep the doors locked. To isolate cars so people cannot move from one to another. For our cars this is justified by our fear of theft. But I do not lock my doors. Perhaps my car is protected by its dustiness and its age of 24 years. But still it is easy to imagine that someone would use the unlockedness of my car for private advantage such as making the car a place to sleep or taking it for a cruz (I don’t leave the keys so it would have to be hot-wired) or taking it somewhere to sell the parts. And yet I keep leaving it unlocked on the street. This is not the work of navitie or stupidity, it is a real move in the world where I ask my neighborhood who’s absoluteness I do not hold (I don’t know everyone and what they want) to respect my vulnerability. Analogous to this with Levinas I welcome the Other into my proximity and with passivity I welcome. When I leave doors unlocked. 

> [To leave oneself open to] the passivity of a trauma, but one that prevents its own representation, a deafening trauma, cutting the thread of consciousness which should have welcomed it in its present, the passivity of being persecuted. (Otherwise, p111)

I leave my car open and this makes possible my car being stolen. If that happens I will be thematized as an idiot for taking the risk. But really I am fighting thematization because I challenge the story that my neighborhood is absolutely thematized as a place where cars are stolen. Analogous to this is that I leave myself open to the Other’s defining of me by my welcome. For Levinas and for me it is absolutely necessary. And for good reason: because that’s how we live together!

### The 2nd reduction: recognizing the intersubjective

I will now introduce the second reduction of Phenomenological ethics: the recognizing of the intersubjective. Others are different from me!

I become self-reflectively aware of and attentive to the other whose perspective calls mine into question and the validity and legitimacy of their own essential, core and pure subjective intentionalities—with respect to the object or state of affairs. (What is…, p4)

Others own cars. I own a car. And yet because my car has been always been in the family, I’ve never purchased another. Although I am like all others who own cars I am different from most because I’ve never purchased a car. For me to have a grip on what it means to purchase a car requires that either I conceptually think it out (go to store pull out wallet?) or I go ask others about what buying a car was like for them. Levinas describes how this process of going to the Other must ALWAYS be done. To know how what buying a car (given that the process is always infinitely deep) means I must go to the Other and ask! I must be aware of the differences between myself and the Other and act accordingly.

#### I take the car to work

I can get to work because of my car - dwelling

I can get to work because of my work. It is a place where I am safe for the elements, soundproof to other people’s voices, where I can feel a sense of completeness. When Levinas refers to event of a person giving themself up entirely to the Other he writes “It is one absolved from every relationship, every game, literally without a situation, without a dwelling place, expelled from everywhere and from itself, one saying to the other "I" or "here I am." (Otherwise, p146) That last part is confusing but the important thing here is that the dwelling is where one is in control. It is necessary to have a dwelling so that one can live. 

It’s important to note that this is not only the physical meaning of the word dwelling. A car will protect you from the elements and a family with love will protect you from the elements too. I consider family and friends to be a necessary part of my dwelling which strangely enough means that I thematize them as instruments for my survival. But that makes sense: I know (I’ve thematized) my brother as someone who will take me into his home and feed me if I need them. I know this as fact! My dwelling allows me to go out into the world, to have a relation with the world.

#### I drive from home to work - the relation

So now that I have have my car and it protects me and comforts me I now go to work. My home I understand but at my work I never know what will be asked of me (at least for this example, there are boring jobs). Analogous to this is how for Levinas the relation connects the I to the Other. This relation exists absolutely and thus can never be silenced (only ignored.) This concept of the ethical relationship is hugely important to Levinas’s entire attack on the excessive conceptualization of Western Philosophy. Jacque Derrida, the first famous reader of Levinas’s work summarized this  the foundation of Levinas’s metaphysics. 

> This thought [reject of Wester calls upon the ethical relationship—a nonviolent relationship to the infinite as infinitely other, to the Other—as the only one capable of opening the space of transcendence and of liberating metaphysics. And does so without supporting ethics and metaphysics by anything other than themselves, and without making them flow into other streams at their source. (Derrida, p102)

All these concepts can be wildly complicated. So for a moment lets reject the car and take the bus instead. 

#### I’d rather take the bus - alterity

There is a bus that can take me to work. There are of course tons of pros and cons to taking the bus. For example the bus takes 4 times as long (bad) but I don’t have to drive (good). But the important thing to recognize here is that when I take the bus I no longer have the shell of the car, 1.5 tonnes of metal, around me. A car is also a weapon so when I take the bus my attack power is reduced to my fists (I have weak little keyboard typing fingers). I’m naked comparatively. Exposed and naked to a bus full of people who I am sharing the space with. And yet when I take the bus rarely do I discourse with my fellow passengers. Its just not done very often. 

There is a difficulty in the bus of saying hello to someone. There is a difficulty in approaching the other in proximity. To get into their space. Why? Because you don’t know who they are. For Levinas it is very important (and must never be forgotten) that the Other has an Alterity, an absolute difference, that can never be thematized. You can’t define other people as perfectly knowable. But that is of course great cause now I get to satisfy my desire for new things in life. Who wants to be bored? No one! Every person has their interest in the world and that interest is insatiable. In the bus I feel uncomfortable because the other person is different from me and yet, and yet that is exactly what I want in life. Everything I know is from my experience with the Other and I want to know more. Is it safer to sit and pout. Yes and that is what I do. But I shouldn’t. 

> Levinas claims that “life is a ﬁne risk to be run” and that those who do not risk the uncertainties, ambiguities, and openness of face-to-face encounters with the ultimately unknown and unknowable other are simply not living the adventure of life, that is, not living the adventure of enjoying and suffering the “good” life. (Simon, p137)

#### Having discourse with the face-to-face

Glory! Instead of suffering in isolation I begin a conversation on the bus. This is possible because I am in proximity with the Other. In my car I could not get into proximity. Even if I was at a stoplight and someone in a car is an actual 3 feet away in the passenger seat there is still the windows, the other noisy cars, focus on driving. When I do speak to someone outside my car from inside my car I feel a shock, a break with my routine, a strangeness. This is for Levinas how I get to the face-to-face relation with the Other: by proximity  analogous to how I lower the window and turn my head towards the Other. Then I say ‘Hi, my name is Emmanuel’. This is very important for Levinas and Phenomenological Ethics because the face-to-face relation requires that I identify myself if that means only saying “here I am,” (Otherwise, p150). I present myself as thematized and then the conversation begins. 

> Not includable in the present, refractory to thematization and representation, the alterity of the neighbor calls for the irreplaceable singularity that lies in me, by accusing this ego, reducing it, in the accusative, to itself. (Otherwise, p153)

#### Use the car to go places during the day

Defining others by their car - thematization as suppression of alterity

It is an often told story that cars define their drivers. The rich car is arrogant and doesn’t mind cutting me off because they are important and have to get to their important meeting on important time. The poor car is reckless and doesn’t care if it gets hit because then ‘it’ get damages. Pick-up trucks have one way of taking left turns that push everyone else off the road and motorcycles are loud, obnoxious, and attention-seeking. In all these cases the person inside the car is forgotten. Analogous to this is the thematization as a suppression the Other’s alterity. To treat someone else as if they are a car is to suppress their alterity so that they are an actual object. An object is finite, knowable, graspable. Like the perfect circle for Plato of the Greeks. For Levinas objectifying the Other pulls everything into a desperate inner monologue. But life is lived with Others so any system of objectification will necessarily fail in so-called ‘special exceptions’. So the motorcyclist is always inconsiderate except for the friend Ruben who is cool except for those times he’s at a red light next to another revving motorcyclist except for that one time he didn’t because of a sleeping baby on a park bench except...

> The models of being and the subject-object correlation, which are justifiable but derivative, do not depict signification, the-one-for-the-other. It indeed shows itself in the said, but does so only after the event, betrayed, foreign to the said of being; it shows itself in it as a contradiction. (Otherwise, p133)


#### Going where I want - taking a bite out of the elemental

Sometimes, if I don’t take the bus to work, I can leave work and get in my car and go get what I want. Somewhere. With the thematized world-view the sandwich that I purchase is a stacking of chemical compounds that my tastebuds receive and then convert into electrical signals. With Levinas I am  living in my subjectivity! That chemical story is just one dry way of looking at it. The truer one is that I am immersed in my using the car to go get the sandwich.

Sensibility is the mode of enjoyment for Levinas – in our enjoyment of things, things revert to the elemental. Our relation to the elements is not one of distance, as required by perception, but one of immersion. Sensation itself is immersed in the elemental medium in which things are found. (Wheeler, Ch3) And importantly, in this case, it means that Others cannot judge the enjoyment of the sandwich-in-itself. My opinion cannot be wrong, misinformed or unworthy. It is and it is great.

#### Sometimes I chauffeur give others - responsibility

So I have a car and with it I can go eat sandwiches aka I can enjoy the world from my dwelling. But what if the Other is suffering. When I ferry other people around its doesn’t matter why they request transport. They come to me suffering and asking me to take them somewhere they want to be. If I choose to be hard I will demand justification, in essence I will demand that I understand in totality their wants. I could claim that I am engaging in reasoned discussion to make sure the wants of the Other are reasonable but for Levinas I am forcing the Other to thematize their being. “Tell me why you need a chauffeur and the reasons better be good.” instead of “Where do you need to go?” When I force the justification I make myself into a objective Being because demanding singularity out of the Other implicitly demands I objectify myself to be an ‘unbiased’ judge. This is precisely what Levinas argues against. For Levinas it is a metaphysical reality that I know my place in the world by my responsibility to Others. That’s how I know who I am, because I am the only person who can get my friend when friend asks.

> But far from innocent platoism or naiveté of premature consciousness, the concern for the other person for Levinas, and contrary to the entire tradition of Western philosophy, is what constitutes our subjectivity. One is a subject only and insofar as one is awakened or ‘sobered up’  to responsibility for the other person. The very identity, what makes me unique. (Filipovic, p59)


#### Choosing where to driving with my car - the apology and reason from discourse

It is often that I am hungry and want to go to a restaurant and the Other is hungry before me and wants to go to a different restaurant. (Please allow the question of which restaurant to go to to stand in place of any question of suffering between the I and the Other) I have the car, the power to respond to the sufferings of the Other and I cannot deny my responsibility. Levinas and Phenomenological Ethics do not allow me to disregard my unique place and just say ‘well I know someone else will get suffering Other around town’. The face-to-face relation exists and now the Other must be answered. 

Standard economic theory has a logic that says that to two agents, knowing what they want as selfish consumers will negotiate by price points, will come a contractual agreement where the result is best possible total happiness (pareto efficiency) If they free to decide they will make the right choice. But for Levinas people are not happiness machines. 

> An order common to the interlocutors is established by the positive act of the one giving the world, his possession, to the other, or by the positive act of the one justifying himself in his freedom before the other, that is, by apology. (Totality, p252)

This means that the I engage the Other in apology for not understanding the Other’s suffering and it’s connection to the restaurant they wish for. The apology means that I command the other to give her ‘world’ to me. I then respond to that world with my world. From the discourse comes an understanding. Reason itself. Reason born not of metaphysics by of ethics.

#### Third Reduction: The introduction of legal metaphysical concepts. 

The first two reductions of Phenomenological Ethics are complete! I have bracketed my subjectivity and I have moved to recognize the perspectives of Others. Now its time to party! Well actually the exact opposite of partying: now its time to sober up and get boring. I am in phenomenological relation with another Being and am to now consider those outside the conversation. How is this done? 

> “...when the perspective of the THIRD, who stands outside the intersubjective relationship, is introduced. This becomes the basis of establishing justice and objective, worldly institutions such as schools, courts, health-care facilities, and governments.” (What is…, p5)

There is ‘common sense’ attitude in cutthroat politics or business that those in the room when the decision gets made get a cut of the deal and those outside the room are left out and get nothing. So if this is the case advantage comes from getting in the room and exclude as many competitors as possible. 

This is totally rejected by Phenomenological Ethics (and any ethical system really). The people outside the room ALWAYS matter and so the question is how are they to be represented? They are not in proximity, they are distance. And thus they will be thematized. Concepts like human rights are built off this: everyone outside the room still gets some benefit of the deal. 

How then to decide the correct thematizations? Firstly there will never be a perfectly correct answer not because of a lack of engineering skills or data accumulation but because there is the infinite alterity of the Other. To speak is to know and to listen is to learn. There can be no justice with perfect answer that only speaks. 

Secondly lets talk about whats happening now and how it can be improved. What is the current way of things? I drive my car to work. Whose suffering are we responding to? The people hurt by my car’s use. How do we currently thematize those Others? “Petrol hurts people’s human rights. Everyone deserves human rights but its their government’s responsibility to provide human rights.” So now we consider a better way to thematize the ‘them’ based on the discourse exchange of worlds. “So they have human rights but because their governments don’t respect those rights I must put my hands on the situation somehow, either directly with the suffering person, through pressure on the legal system, or by refusing to drive my car.” I will walk to work, my intentions can lie by my feet cannot. 

Phenomenological Ethics in this scenario asks me to walk to work. But I can’t (I’m sharing my world) because it takes too long. I need to return to the question of what to do. I need to ask others, like you the reader: What do you think I should do?

#### Putting gas in the car 

The pricing system - reduction of the Other to a theme

When I purchase the gas I hold in consideration only the question of ‘best value’ measured absolutely by my wallet. Though I have no malice in my intention to buy gas I could be actually hurting someone. Its possible and given the violence near petro wealth on the globe even probable. Other people cannot necessarily just defend themselves.

But the other can be murdered by the very force I possess in spite of the resistant force with which she may oppose me. She resists as absolutely other and can oppose the force that strikes her, not with a force of resistance but with the very unforseeableness of her reation. (Simon, p139)

When I execute the third reduction of Phenomenological Ethics I need to do a better job than just objectifying the Third as a price point! This currently is the way of neo-classical economic theory. Which of course makes me sad. 

I desire to I know who’s hurt by my car - the signification of other’s work

*There is only so much space in the world. I must take some of the space with my body. I have no other way to live. But how much space do I take up elsewhere? With my 1990 Accord I don’t take up as much space as a full size pickup. Yet I take up enough space that someone could put half a small apartment, a home, wherever my car is. I also breath in air to live but my car takes in 1000s of times more. In both these cases Others are suffering by lack of housing or raising tides from global warming. For Levinas there is no breaking the substitution I have for the Other’s suffering. For what? A car? There is suffering caused by me that is useless and unnecessary.*

*I have among my friends narratives that proclaim global warming to be a hoax, or too costly, or “if people care they can do something about it for themselves.” (I swear these friends are friendly in other different dimensions). By my narrative they are blind to the useless suffering.*

But for Levinas, even the “sins” of environmental injustice are, at their very core, a consequence of not coming to terms with the issue of “useless suffering” and the ways in which we calculate or give accounts for the suffering of others. (Simon, p140)


#### My car doesn’t break - illeity?

I don’t really understand what ‘illeity’ is. My best guess is that things will turn out well if one relaxes and just become another among Others. My car has by its continued functioning and never breaking down kept me in the financial position to study philosophy. What did I do to deserve such luck? I don’t know if someone else is just looking out for me. It is likely that the workers who made the car were proud of their workmanship and so their goodwill to me, the impossible to know in proximity Third was an act of illeity. What did I do to deserve such good attention? Nothing really, I was 7 years old at the time. The organization did something for me as a thematized Third. And I really appreciate it. 

Or maybe I just got lucky. That happens too right?

#### My need to sleep - My car causes suffering for Others. 

I am convinced that my car is cause others to suffer. A natural retort could be that sometimes suffering is ok because of immeasurable greatness brought on by works of art or beauty. Isn’t it ok that Michelangelo painted the Sistine Chapel ceiling with funds that could have been used to feed the homeless? No. 

“Levinas rejects justifying suffering for the sake of a “kingdom of transcendent ends” that would guide one by a benevolent wisdom, rejecting all grand designs and any suprasensible project, including those involving an arbitrary god, belief in progress, a metaphysics of original sin, or a congenital failure.” (Simon, p143)

The story that Michelangelo knew by perfect knowledge that his painting was more important than the Other’s suffering is illegitimate. The story that Michelangelo prayed that his work would best respond to the suffering of the Other is a different better story. What would it mean if a child walked onto his work site and he gave the child a silver coin. Imagine after looking up at the ceiling the other child gives the silver coin back? A much better story. Phenomenological Ethics as the understanding that inside cars are people of total alterity. Or perhaps that outside people are sometimes cars and that you can’t describe the inside with the outside.

## Conclusions

I just wrote my final paper on Levinas by describing Levinas using a car. That’s pretty cool. My Honda was just car but not any more. Its a explication tool! Never saw that coming. I just used it to introduce and discuss key terms from Levinas and the three reductions of Phenomenological Ethics including  the same “I don’t clean my car”, the other “I never know who’s parked around me”, thematization as a constant “The car is not looking good”, thematization “either I’m clean or I’m not”, welcome “I don’t lock my car”, dwelling “I can get to work because of my car”, the relation “I drive from home to work”, the alterity “the experience of the bus”, the face-to-face “Having discourse at work”, thematization as suppression of alterity, “Defining others by their car”, taking a bite out of the elemental “Going where I want”, discourse “Sometimes I give others transpotation", the apology and reason from discourse “Choosing where to driving with my car”, The pricing system - reduction of the Other to a theme “Putting gas in the car”, the signification of other’s suffering “I desire to I know who’s hurt by my car cause my car needs air and parking spaces”, Illeity? “My car doesn’t break”,  My need to sleep “My car causes suffering for Others”.
